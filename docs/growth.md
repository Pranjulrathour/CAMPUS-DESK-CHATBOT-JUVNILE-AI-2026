# Growth Plan: Campus Doubt Desk

Based on `prd.md`. This file tells us one thing: is the bot really helping students, or not?

---

## 1. The most important metric

**Doubts solved without going to the office.**

Out of every 10 questions asked, at least 6 should get a 👍 ("Yes, this solved my doubt").

- **Target:** 60% or more
- **Why this one:** The real problem is the crowded office. If students get their answer from the bot, they don't need to stand in the line.
- **Reality check:** Mrs. Sharma keeps a simple tally of handbook questions at the office, 1 week before launch and 1 week after. We want **30% fewer** of these questions.

## 2. Three smaller metrics

| Metric | What it tells us | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thumbs-up rate** (out of every 10 votes, how many are 👍) | Are the answers good? | 8 out of 10 |
| **Answer time** | Is the bot fast enough? | Under 10 seconds |
| **"I don't know" rate** (how often the bot says "I don't know") | Is the bot missing things, or is the handbook missing things? | Less than 15% of questions |

**Bonus learning:** If many students ask the same question and the bot says "I don't know", the problem is probably the handbook, not the bot. That is useful feedback for the college.

## 3. How we will measure (simple way)

- **The bot counts:** questions asked, 👍 and 👎 clicks, "I don't know" answers, and answer time. No names or personal data are saved.
- **Office tally sheet:** Mrs. Sharma marks a line on paper for every handbook question at the counter.
- **Weekly check:** Every Monday, the PM looks at the numbers for 15 minutes and reads every 👎 answer.

## 4. Three experiments we can try

| # | Experiment | How | We win if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Short vs detailed answers** | Alternate days: 2-line answers one day, 5-line answers the next | One style gets clearly more 👍 |
| 2 | **Suggested questions: show or hide** | Show 4-5 suggested questions for one week, hide them the next | More students ask at least one question when they are shown |
| 3 | **WhatsApp link vs QR poster** | Share a separate link in the batch WhatsApp group and a QR poster near the office | We learn which one brings more students, and do more of that |

Run one experiment at a time, for at least 1 week each.

## 5. Launch plan for the first 50 students

| When | What we do |
| --- | --- |
| **Before launch** | Office staff pass the 20-question test (Phase 4 of the roadmap) |
| **Day 1** | Start with one 1st-year batch. The class rep shares the link in the batch WhatsApp group with 3 example questions. A QR poster goes up at the office counter and on the hostel notice board. |
| **Day 1 onwards** | When students come to the office with a simple handbook question, Mrs. Sharma says: "Try this first, it shows the page number too." |
| **Day 3** | Check the numbers, read every 👎 answer, fix the top problems |
| **Day 7** | Goal: 50 students have used it. Talk to 5 of them for 5 minutes each: what worked, what confused them. |
| **Day 14** | Decide: open it to all 1st years, fix more, or stop |

## 6. When we will decide the product has failed

**Pause right away if:** a wrong answer from the bot causes real harm (for example, a student misses a fee deadline), until we find and fix the cause.

**Call it failed at Day 30 if any one of these is true (even after fixes):**

- Thumbs-up rate is below 5 out of 10 (50%)
- The office tally shows no drop in handbook questions
- Fewer than 20 of the first 50 students came back to ask a second question

Failed does not mean wasted. We write down what we learned (wrong problem, wrong channel, or bad answers) and decide the next step from there.
